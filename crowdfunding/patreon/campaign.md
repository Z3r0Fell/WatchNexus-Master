## Why Patreon **after** Kickstarter?

- Patreon is **subscription-flavoured**. Asking the same audience to
  back BOTH a one-time Kickstarter AND a recurring Patreon at launch
  splits their pledge intent.
- After Kickstarter closes, evangelists who want to keep supporting
  WatchNexus monthly have a place to land.
- Patreon's algorithm rewards **steady recurring posts**, which
  aligns with the post-launch v1.1 dev cycle, not the pre-launch
  marketing push.

## Patreon-specific posting cadence

Patreon's algorithm rewards regular paid-tier-only posts. Plan:

- **2 paid posts per month minimum** (subscribers-only)
- **1 free post per month** (public, used to attract new backers)

Suggested editorial calendar:

| Month | Topic |
|---|---|
| Month 1 (just-launched) | Behind the scenes: how the 11-Docker-container origin story collapsed to one binary |
| Month 2 | Deep dive: how the Fortress Protocol works |
| Month 3 | Deep dive: hardware-transcoding profile detection in Strudel |
| Month 4 | "What's coming in v1.1" (paid) + roadmap voting (free) |
| Month 5 | Q&A roundup — most-asked questions from the past 6 months |
| Month 6 | Lessons from running a solo software business for 6 months |

## Charge cadence

- Patreon defaults to **charging on the 1st** of every month.
- Some creators charge per-creation (only when they post). **Don't**
  do that — your backers want predictable monthly support, not
  guess-when-Auz-charges-me.

## Cross-platform reconciliation

Patreon backers are logged in the Open Collective ledger as
non-financial contributors with their cumulative pledge total. This
keeps the "single source of truth" view consistent.

## Operational mechanics

### Fee structure

- Patreon "Lite" plan: 5% platform fee + 2.9% + $0.30/transaction
  Stripe = ~8%
- Patreon "Pro" plan ($24/mo): 8% platform fee. Worth it after ~50
  monthly backers (gives you analytics tools)
- Patreon "Premium" plan: 12% platform fee. Adds a CRM. Not worth
  it for solo creators.

### Tax handling

- Patreon issues 1099-K if US income exceeds $20K/yr or 200
  transactions
- Canadian creators (Auz) don't receive a 1099 but Patreon sends a
  manual annual summary. Report on Canadian T1.

## Anti-mistakes

1. **Don't ladle Patreon money into operational mistakes.** Recurring
   revenue is precious because it's predictable. Spend it on
   recurring costs (hosting, signing renewals), not one-off
   purchases.
2. **Don't promise more than 2 paid posts a month** at launch. Start
   light; you can always over-deliver. Patreon creators who burn out
   under their own posting commitments lose backers fast.
3. **Don't gate the core product behind Patreon.** A Pro licence
   bought through Patreon's monthly tier earns the same way it does
   through Kickstarter. No "Patreon exclusive" features — only
   exclusive *posts* and Discord roles.
